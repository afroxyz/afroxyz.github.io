Form design / Service-design study

A simpler decision about garden waste collections

Separating the bins a resident owns from the bins they want collected, and making the payment period clear before they pay.

Reconstructed prototype · outcomes unmeasured

Help residents pay for the collection they need

I separated bins owned from bins to be collected and put the price and collection period together before payment. The aim was to reduce incorrect orders and billing queries.

The design problem

Owning two garden waste bins does not mean a resident wants to pay for both to be collected. A form that treats those numbers as interchangeable can lead people towards the wrong purchase.

The journey also needs to explain that a payment covers collections until March 2027. The price remains the same regardless of the payment date.

The decisions

Ask about the service being purchased

Ask how many bins the resident wants collected. Keep that distinct from how many bins they already own, including any they have ordered. Residents can choose fewer bins for collection than they own.

Explain the prerequisites before payment

Residents must have a garden waste bin before using the service. If they need additional bins, they must order them first. Properties with shared or communal bins are not eligible for this service.

Keep the price and period together

The content puts £60 for each bin next to the collection period. It avoids treating a late payment as the start of a fresh year’s subscription.

The trade-off

A single-bin route can feel quicker, while a multiple-bin route needs a clearer count and price. The shared content still has to explain the same collection period and prerequisites. Branching should reduce irrelevant questions without hiding those essentials.

What this study demonstrates

A clearer purchase decision, an explicit service period and a distinction between ownership and subscription. These are design decisions; this page does not claim measured changes in conversion or collection outcomes.
