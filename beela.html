Beela: content strategy for a donation flow

Beela is a Swedish nonprofit started by Brazilian immigrants Juliana Araújo and Denise Muniz, helping women and non-binary people who are immigrants get into jobs in Swedish tech.

Role
Lead UX writer in a team of 5
Tools
Miro, Figma, Notion, Slack
Team
4 UX writers, 6 designers, 4 researchers, 1 product manager
Timeline
8+ weeks

Communicating the value proposition is the most important part of the flow. Donors will not give to something they cannot understand.

Overview

The core task of this phase was creating copy for the donation flow. Since this was the final phase before the website went live, the writing team decided to conduct a content audit and usability testing.

Problem

Donating to non-profit organisations is much harder than spending on e-commerce sites. The writing team and I were tasked with communicating Beela's value proposition to attract volunteers and online donations. That meant building trust with potential donors, reflecting Beela's personality through voice and tone, and keeping the voice consistent through the donation flow while using the right tone for each stage of the user journey.

Solution

  • Collaborated with designers and product managers to create copy for the donation flow.
  • Performed an audit, inventory and taxonomy of content.
  • Implemented changes based on findings from usability testing.
  • Used tone mapping to define Beela's voice and tone across contexts.

Usability testing

The research team created 3 tasks for the usability tests, as well as open-ended interview questions. The tasks focused on how users engaged with the donation page prototype, how easy it was to find information, and their expectations of various sections. Participants also rated how easy they found each task and explained their ratings. Three issues emerged:

  1. Users wanted to know what Beela does and for whom before they commit to donating.
  2. Users wanted transparency about how their donations would be used. The lack of detailed information about Beela's work would likely affect donations.
  3. Some participants were unclear about what Beela offers, with some interpreting it as only a career mentoring programme. Some also missed the focus on "Swedish" and "tech".

From findings to a content strategy

Reviewing the findings, we realised that communicating Beela's value proposition was the most important aspect of the flow. Our initial prototype didn't fully pay attention to the copy, which is arguably the most important part of getting users to donate. We failed to highlight Beela's activities and how donations would support the cause, so there wasn't enough transparency about what was happening with the money.

The writing team zeroed in on the content strategy, starting by listing everything Beela has to offer. We collaborated with the research team to work out what worked and what didn't. We asked questions about the messaging and simplified the words to create more context, and noted how each section of the page made participants feel. That helped us isolate the copy that really resonated.

Decisions

  • Create an FAQ page to address pressing questions from users and about events.
  • Create copy on how donations would be used.
  • Rephrase copy on the mentorship page to highlight Beela's activities.
  • Collaborate with the design team to create an events page so users can see what activities Beela organises.
  • Add a testimonials page, shed more light on the mentorship and apprenticeship programme, and rework the mission statement.

We also realised there are other ways people could support Beela's cause besides sending financial donations. We brainstormed this with the product team and the client, and argued on the writing team that it would add more legitimacy to Beela's cause.

What I take from this

Copy was not a finishing layer on the donation flow. It was the mechanism that determined whether anyone donated. The usability tests told us where meaning was missing, and the content audit told us what to say.
