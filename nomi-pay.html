Designing contextual AI support for a payment plan

A fictional pay-over-time product I created to explore how content design and conversational AI can work together in a financial service.

Help customers resolve payment questions in-app

Nomi Pay is a fictional product. It is an independent concept project, not client work.

Overview

The project focuses on a customer with one £150 instalment remaining who wants to know whether she can move the payment date. I designed the payment journey, the conversational experience and the agent behaviour, then built a working prototype in Lovable to test how the experience handled context, different ways of asking the same question, restrictions, and recovery when the customer's preferred action was unavailable.

Problem

Payment information can be technically correct without answering the question a customer actually has. A customer may be able to see what they owe and when it is due, but still need to understand whether they can change the payment, why an option is unavailable, what will happen next, and what they can do instead. The challenge was to reduce that uncertainty without making customers search through policy content, repeat information the product already knew, or rely on an AI assistant that could guess beyond the available evidence.

Solution

I put the payment amount and due date on the plan screen and carried that context into Nomi Assistant. The assistant uses the selected payment to check the rule and explain the result. If changing the date is unavailable, it offers support options for that plan.

Customer scenario

Maya, 31, bought a £600 laptop using Nomi Pay's four-instalment plan. She has paid three instalments. Her final £150 is due in 3 days. She opens the app to ask whether she can move the date. She is not in financial distress, but wants flexibility because another bill is due first. Nomi already knows the purchase, remaining balance, next payment amount, due date and current plan status, so the customer should not have to repeat information the product already holds.

Product constraint

  • Payment dates cannot be changed when the payment is due within 3 days.
  • The assistant checks this rule against the due date of the payment in view.
  • The assistant must not invent an exception.

Pain points became design constraints

I identified five mental barriers: an unclear payment position; uncertainty about what they can change; "can I trust the AI assistant's answer?"; dead-end answers; and repeating information the product already knows. Rather than treating content as a layer added after the interface, I used these barriers to decide what customers needed to know, when they needed to know it and what the product should do next.

Reducing cognitive effort

Remaining balance, progress, payment amount and date, with contextual help, are primary information on the plan screen.

Preserving context and agency

The assistant entry carries payment context into the conversation, accepts natural language and separates asking from acting.

Explaining outcomes and supporting recovery

The "You can't move this payment" screen leads with the outcome, translates system rules into customer language and gives another useful route forward.

Making system activity visible

The eligibility check screen shows Nomi is checking, using verified payment state and grounding the answer in product rules.

Building the prototype

I built the working prototype in Lovable so I could move beyond static screens and test the experience as a conversation: could customers understand their payment situation, ask for help in their own words and get a clear, relevant answer grounded in information the product already holds? I also tested whether Nomi preserved context, recognised different ways of expressing the same need, clarified ambiguity when necessary, explained a restriction in plain language and helped the customer recover.

Annotated payment conversation

Context and conversation state

The customer is viewing a known payment plan. That context carries into the assistant, so the design can resolve which payment a question refers to and check the relevant rule before answering.

The model below is reconstructed for this portfolio from the documented prototype behaviour. The check supplies the outcome and a reason code such as payment_due_within_3_days. Nomi turns that result into an explanation and a useful next step.

  1. Plan visibleOne £150 payment remains, due in three days.
  2. Request understoodResolve the date-change intent. Clarify a consequential ambiguity.
  3. Eligibility checkedThe simulated rule returns eligible: false.
  4. Restriction explainedExplain the outcome and show an available next step.

Conversation patterns

Answer a contextual question
Identify the intent and payment → check the rule → explain the result → offer a relevant next step.
Clarify an ambiguous request
Check whether the previous turn resolves the reference → ask one focused question if needed → resume with the confirmed intent.
Handle missing or conflicting data
Say what can be confirmed → pause the eligibility decision → offer a way to check the payment details.

“Can I change it?” can refer to the payment date or the payment method. If the previous turn establishes the date, Nomi can keep going. If both meanings are plausible, Nomi can ask “Do you want to change the date of your next payment or the card you pay with?”

Handling multiple intents

Worked design extension

“When is my next payment, and can I move it?”

Resolve both questions against the same instalment. Give the amount and due date, then explain the eligibility result and offer support. A possible response is “Your £150 payment is due on 20 October, in 3 days. You can’t move it this close to the due date. I can show you your payment support options.”

A message such as “Can I move it? I can’t afford it this week” adds a support need. The proposed pattern acknowledges that concern, explains the verified payment state, and prioritises the approved support route in the same response.

Action and authority boundaries

The conversation separates a question about a payment from permission to alter it. In this scenario, the date-change request reaches an ineligible result. The prototype then supports understanding and recovery.

Check eligibilityShow a checking state, then explain the simulated rule’s result.The response is grounded in the known payment state.

Recovery, handoff and escalation

The prototype offers payment support options inside the product. Maya can view her schedule or see what happens if a payment fails. This gives her a place to continue after an ineligible result.

For a production service, I’d design a separate escalation path for a customer who says they cannot pay, asks for a person, or reaches a point where the assistant cannot verify the payment or resolve the issue.

Offer the right route
Explain why the assistant is offering support, what the team can help with and how to reach it. Use a route approved for the customer’s situation.
Carry the right context
If assisted transfer is available, show what would be passed on, such as the payment reference and reason for contact. Follow the service’s consent and data-sharing rules.
Report what happened
Confirm transfer only after the receiving system acknowledges it. If it fails or is unavailable, explain that state and offer a verified contact option.

Proposed evaluation for the extended design

I’d evaluate the full conversation against a known payment state, including what the assistant says, what it offers and whether any action actually occurs. The cases below define expected behaviour for a future run.

ScenarioPass criteria
Eligibility boundary at 4, 3 and 2 daysCheck the same rule across all three fixtures. Four days meets the date criterion. Three and two days do not. A question alone leaves every payment unchanged.
Two intents in one messageAnswer both the due-date and date-change questions using the same payment. Avoid asking for information already on the plan.
Ambiguous “Can I change it?”Resolve the reference from a clear prior turn. Ask one focused question when the date and payment method are both plausible.
Missing or conflicting payment detailsState what cannot be confirmed, withhold the eligibility decision and give an approved way to check the details.

I’d treat an unauthorised change, an invented payment rule or a false claim that a handoff succeeded as a critical failure. Once those checks pass, I’d score intent coverage, clarity of the reason and usefulness of the next step.

Usability testing

I tested the working prototype with 4 participants against three research goals:

  1. Test payment comprehension. Can customers quickly identify how much is left to pay and when the next payment is due without searching or calculating?
  2. Test how the agent handles ambiguity. If someone says "Can I change it?", can Nomi use the existing payment context when the meaning is clear, but ask a focused clarification when it is not?
  3. Test recovery conversational turns. When the preferred action is unavailable, does the conversation help customers understand what they can do next rather than leaving them at a dead end?

The four-participant exercise documents the research scope. Participant findings, revision traces and production service measures remain to be published.

Learnings

  1. Context reduces unnecessary conversation. When the assistant already knows which payment the customer is viewing, asking them to repeat that information adds friction rather than clarity.
  2. Natural language needs more than one "correct" phrase. Customers may say "move my payment", "pay later", "push it back" or "change the date". The experience needs to respond to the underlying need rather than force customers to learn product terminology.
  3. Clarification is useful only when ambiguity matters. A conversational agent should use available context when the meaning is clear, but ask a focused question when there are genuinely several possible interpretations.
  4. A negative answer still needs to help. "You can't move this payment" is not enough on its own. Explaining why and giving a useful next step turns a refusal into a recoverable experience.
  5. Confidence should match what the product knows. The assistant should make a definite statement only when the relevant payment information has been checked. When information is unavailable, acknowledging uncertainty is better than guessing.

Next steps

  1. Test with customers to see whether people understand their payment position and make sense of Nomi's responses without additional explanation.
  2. Test more ambiguous language such as "Can I change it?", "I need more time" or "What can I do?".
  3. Define a fuller support journey. The prototype deliberately limits support options rather than inventing policies. A production version needs agreed rules and content for missed payments, failed payments and financial difficulty.
  4. Test edge cases and failure states: payment data unavailable, conflicting or outdated, or an action that cannot be completed.
  5. Measure service outcomes. Track correct understanding of payment status, completion of the next useful step and repeat contacts for the same issue. Review unresolved conversations alongside self-service rates to understand where customers still need support.
