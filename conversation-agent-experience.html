Work with me / 03

Conversational AI & agent experience design

For teams exploring chatbots, support assistants or AI services that need to understand requests and guide people through consequential tasks.

Discuss a project

The starting point

When this helps

  • A chatbot answers questions but struggles to keep context or help someone complete a task.
  • Unclear intents, restrictions or handoffs leave users repeating themselves or reaching a dead end.
  • The team needs to define what an agent may do and how its behaviour will be tested.

I would begin with the customer’s task and the information and service rules needed to complete it. Mapping conversation state alongside the service journey makes the context, actions and handoffs explicit before the dialogue is refined.

A defined piece of work

What you would receive

Use-case and journey definition
The user needs, supported tasks, service constraints and success criteria for the experience.
Conversation architecture
Intent and state maps, context requirements, tool-action boundaries and routes to human support.
Prototype dialogue and interaction
A testable conversation covering useful responses, clarification, restrictions, recovery and confirmation.
Evaluation specification
A scenario set and assessment criteria for task success, grounding, tool use, escalation and failure handling.

Designing for the consequences

Rules, review and collaboration

The prototype would make consequential actions visible, request confirmation at the right point and provide recovery or escalation when a task cannot continue. I would work with product, engineering and service specialists to agree the rules and test the behaviour.

Discovery, architecture, prototyping and evaluation planning. Production integrations and deployment would be developed with engineering and service specialists.

Built for the team to use

Handover and measuring value

Your team would receive the conversation maps, prototype, dialogue specifications, action and escalation rules, and evaluation scenarios. A review session would identify what engineering, service operations and content teams need to implement and maintain the experience.

Evaluation would examine task completion, correct use of context, unsupported answers, action confirmation and successful handoffs. Production measures could then include resolution, repeat contact and recovery from failed requests.
